# I Ching 64 Hexagrams

The I Ching (易经, Book of Changes) is an ancient Chinese divination text based on 64 hexagrams (八卦), each composed of 6 lines (yao) that can be either broken (yin) or solid (yang).

## Core Concepts

### The 64 Hexagrams

Each hexagram has:
- **Name** (卦名): Traditional Chinese name
- **Symbol** (符号): The 6-line structure
- **Judgment** (彖): Overall meaning
- **Image** (象): The symbolic imagery
- **Lines** (爻): Meanings of each changing line

### The Eight Trigrams (八卦)

The building blocks of hexagrams:
- ☰ **乾 (Qian)** - Creative, Heaven, Force
- ☱ **兑 (Dui)** - Joy, Lake, Open
- ☲ **离 (Li)** - Clinging, Fire, Radiance
- ☳ **震 (Zhen)** - Arousing, Thunder, Motion
- ☴ **巽 (Xun)** - Penetrating, Wind, Gentle
- ☵ **Kan (坎)** - Abyss, Water, Danger
- ☶ **艮 (Gen)** - Keeping Still, Mountain, Rest
- ☷ **坤 (Kun)** - Receptive, Earth, Field

## Divination Method

### Coin Toss Method
1. Hold 3 coins (yin side = 2, yang side = 3)
2. Toss 6 times, recording each result
3. From bottom to top, draw the hexagram
4. Note any changing lines (6 or 9)

### Changing Lines
- **6 (Old Yin)**: Changes from broken to solid, becomes opposite
- **9 (Old Yang)**: Changes from solid to broken, becomes opposite

## Interpretation Guidelines

1. **Read the Judgment first** - Overall meaning of the hexagram
2. **Consider the Image** - Symbolic representation
3. **Examine changing lines** - Dynamic elements
4. **Note trigram composition** - Upper and lower trigrams
5. **Apply to situation** - Practical application
